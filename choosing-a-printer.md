# Choosing a Printer

There are hundreds of printers. Here's what actually matters, in the
order that matters.

## What to look for

**Build volume.** The biggest object you can print — commonly around
220 × 220 × 250 mm. That's plenty for household parts, toys, and
brackets. Bigger is not better unless you have a specific large project;
big printers take more space and longer to heat.

**Automatic bed leveling.** The printer probes the build plate and
compensates for tilt on its own. This used to be a premium feature;
now it's common even on budget machines. Worth having — manual leveling
is the fiddliest part of the hobby.

**Direct drive extruder.** The feed motor sits on the print head.
Better for flexible filament (TPU), slightly heavier print head. Most
new printers in 2025–2026 are direct drive; it's a plus, not a must.

**Enclosure.** A box around the printer that holds in heat. You need
one for ABS/ASA (they warp and smell without it). You don't need one
for PLA or PETG. Some printers sell an enclosure as an add-on — fine
to add later if you outgrow PLA.

**Multi-color / multi-material.** Systems that print in several colors
or materials in one job (filament changers, tool changers). Fun, but
they add cost, waste filament on purging, and multiply the things that
can go wrong. Learn single-color first.

## What not to overthink

**Print speed claims.** Manufacturers advertise ever-faster speeds.
Fast printing is real, but quality at top speed varies, and most of
your prints will run at moderate speeds anyway. Don't pick a printer
on the speed number alone.

**Resolution numbers.** Nearly every FDM printer can do 0.1 mm layers.
The differences between printers show up in reliability and ease of
use, not in the spec sheet's smallest number.

## The honest price talk

A $200 printer melts PLA about as well as a $600 printer. What the
extra money buys is convenience: auto-leveling, quieter motors, a
better screen, fewer assembly headaches, better support. If you enjoy
tinkering, budget printers are genuinely good now. If you want it to
just work, pay for the convenience features, not for a bigger spec
sheet.

Buy for the filament you plan to use: printing only PLA and PETG needs
nothing fancy. Planning ABS parts or multi-color? That's when the
enclosure and the filament system earn their price.

## Where people actually buy

Manufacturer stores, Amazon, and hobby retailers. Check return policies
before ordering — printers are heavy and occasionally arrive damaged.
Community reviews from actual owners (Reddit's r/3Dprinting, YouTube
makers) are worth more than any spec sheet.
