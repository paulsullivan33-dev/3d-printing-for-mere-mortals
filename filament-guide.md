# Filament in Plain Language

Filament is plastic string on a spool. The type of plastic decides how
easy it prints, how strong the part is, and where you can use it. Start
with PLA. Add others when you have a reason.

## The four you'll meet first

**PLA** — the beginner plastic. Prints around 200°C on a 60°C bed, no
enclosure needed, barely smells, sticks well. Downsides: brittle
(snaps rather than bends) and softens in a hot car (~60°C). Perfect for
decorations, organizers, prototypes, toys. If you're unsure, use PLA.

**PETG** — tougher PLA, roughly. Prints around 230–240°C, handles heat
better (won't melt in the car), bends instead of snapping. Slightly
stringier and fussier about settings. Good for functional parts that
live indoors or outdoors: brackets, hooks, enclosures.

**TPU** — flexible, rubber-like plastic. Prints slowly (~220–230°C)
and wants a direct-drive extruder. Great for phone cases, gaskets,
feet, anything that should squish. Start with harder TPU (95A) before
trying the squishy stuff.

**ABS / ASA** — strong, heat-resistant engineering plastics (~240–260°C).
They shrink as they cool, so they warp and crack without an enclosure,
and they smell — ventilate. ASA is ABS's weather-resistant sibling,
good for outdoor parts. Don't start here; graduate to it.

## The practical bits

**Dryness matters.** Filament absorbs moisture from the air, and wet
filament prints badly (popping, stringing, rough surfaces). PETG and
TPU are thirstier than PLA. Store opened spools in sealed bags or a dry
box with desiccant. If prints suddenly get ugly, damp filament is a
prime suspect.

**Diameter and weight.** Standard is 1.75 mm filament on 1 kg spools.
Your slicer assumes 1.75 mm — don't buy 2.85 mm by accident.

**Brands.** There are dozens; quality varies more by batch than by
logo. When you find a spool that prints well, note the brand — and
re-tune temperature when you switch, even within the same plastic type.

**Safety.** PLA and PETG are low-odor and fine in a ventilated room.
ABS/ASA need ventilation or an enclosure with filtration — the fumes
are unpleasant and not something to breathe all day. Never leave any
printer running completely unattended; it's a 200°C+ heating element.
