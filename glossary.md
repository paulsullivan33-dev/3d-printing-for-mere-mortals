# Glossary

Quick definitions, A to Z. Terms point to the guide that explains
them in context.

**ABS** — strong, heat-resistant filament that warps without an
enclosure. See [Filament](filament-guide.md).

**Adhesion** — how well the first layer grips the build plate. Most
print failures start here.

**ASA** — ABS's weather-resistant sibling, good for outdoor parts.

**Bed leveling / tramming** — making the build plate parallel to the
nozzle's movement. Auto bed leveling does it with a probe.

**Bowden** — extruder style where the feed motor sits on the frame and
pushes filament through a tube. See [How It Works](how-3d-printing-works.md).

**Brim** — a flat rim printed around the part's base for extra grip.
Snaps off after.

**Build plate / bed** — the surface the object prints on, usually
heated.

**Build volume** — the maximum printable size, e.g. 220 × 220 × 250 mm.

**Direct drive** — extruder style with the feed motor on the print
head. Better for flexible filament.

**Enclosure** — a box around the printer holding in heat. Needed for
ABS/ASA.

**Extruder** — the mechanism that grips filament and feeds it into the
hot end.

**FDM** — fused deposition modeling: building objects from melted
plastic layers. The home-printer method.

**Filament** — plastic string on a spool, the raw material. Usually
1.75 mm diameter.

**G-code** — the move-by-move instructions the slicer generates for
the printer.

**Hot end** — the assembly that melts filament: heater, nozzle, and
cooling.

**Infill** — the interior pattern (often honeycomb or grid). 15–20% is
typical; parts aren't printed solid.

**Layer height** — thickness of each printed layer. 0.2 mm is the
everyday default.

**Nozzle** — the tiny tip plastic squeezes through, usually 0.4 mm.
A wear item; replaceable and cheap.

**Overhang** — part of the model sticking out sideways past ~45°.
Needs supports or reorienting.

**PEI** — a build-plate surface coating with excellent adhesion for
common plastics.

**PETG** — tough, slightly flexible filament for functional parts.

**PLA** — the beginner filament: easy, low-odor, brittle.

**Raft** — a plastic mat printed under the whole part. Rarely needed
on modern printers.

**Retraction** — the slicer pulling filament back during travel moves
to prevent stringing.

**Slicer** — software converting 3D models into G-code. Examples:
OrcaSlicer, PrusaSlicer, Cura, Bambu Studio.

**Stringing** — thin plastic hairs between parts, from oozing during
travel.

**Supports** — temporary scaffolding for overhangs, removed after
printing.

**TPU** — flexible, rubber-like filament.

**Walls / perimeters** — the solid outer shells of a print. More walls
beat more infill for strength.

**Warping** — corners lifting off the bed as plastic cools and
shrinks.

**Z-offset** — the fine nozzle-gap adjustment above the bed. Set with
the paper-drag test.
